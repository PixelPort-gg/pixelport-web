Flow Of War just reached our top compatibility tier: **verified**.

It is a strategy game that mixes base building with wave defense. Your workers gather resources and run the economy on their own, so your attention stays on holding the line. It ships for Windows only, with no Mac build.

## What we tested

A real Mac ran it. We played roughly 25 minutes on an M1 Pro: it launched straight away, rendered cleanly, held a smooth framerate, and saved progress properly across a restart. There was nothing to configure: no launch flags, no compatibility toggles, no workarounds.

Two details make this one unusually clean. The game needs no Steam client running in the background, and it ships no DRM, so it starts directly into the game.

We are telling you the length of the session on purpose. Verified means a person sat down and played it, not that a script watched it reach a menu. It also means we have not played all 30 campaign levels. If something breaks deeper in, we want to hear about it, and we will change the tier if it turns out we were wrong.

## The developer sent us the key

Rongcon Games supplied the key we tested with, and Jack, the developer, is in our Discord. That matters more than it might sound. Most of our testing is done on games bought at retail, with no line back to the people who made them. When a developer hands us a key and reads the reports, problems that would normally end as a shrug on a forum can end as a fix instead.

If you play it on a Mac, tell us how it goes. Jack is reading.

## What the tiers mean

Every game on Pixel Port carries a tier so you know what to expect before you install: verified (a Mac ran it end to end), playable (graded but not yet hand-verified), needs attention (runs with caveats), and unsupported (a hard blocker such as kernel anti-cheat). Flow Of War just moved to the top of that list.
